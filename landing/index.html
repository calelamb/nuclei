QEC results, with the working shown.

Point Nuclei at the sinter CSVs and Stim circuits you already have. It turns them into statistics you can defend: rates with their units, Λ fits that say what they left out, and decoders compared with their intervals.

Open a CSV in your browser Desktop app

Free and open source under MIT. No account, no install for the browser version.

1Bring the files you have

No new format and no import script. Nuclei reads sinter's own CSV, groups rows by their metadata, and merges repeated runs of the same point.

In the browser the file is read in your tab. Nothing is uploaded.

2Read it correctly

Every plot states what its y-axis counts, per shot or per round, and every point carries its interval. Thin statistics look thin.

The d = 9 point at p = 0.1% rests on 33 errors. Its bar says so: ±34%.

3Fit Λ, and say what was left out

Λ is fitted from per-round rates. Points above threshold, where the logical error rate saturates at one half, are listed as dropped instead of quietly skipped.

Per shot, the curves in Figure 2 cross near 0.4%. Per round, Λ is still 1.4 there. Units matter.

4Compare decoders without fooling yourself

Put two decoders side by side on the same points, each with its interval, before you call one of them better.

Open a single decoded shot on the detector graph to see the matching the decoder chose.
